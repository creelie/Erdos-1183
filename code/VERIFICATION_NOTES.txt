Independent verification performed before submission (September 2026)
=======================================================================

This note documents the checks performed on the accompanying paper's
computational claims (Theorem 1.4, "Exact values for small n") before
the paper was formatted and submitted, over and above the checks the
paper itself already describes.

1. brute_small.py was re-run from scratch (no SAT solver) and reproduced
   f(n), F(n) for n = 1,2,3,4 exactly as printed in the paper:
     f: 1, 2, 2, 3
     F: 1, 2, 2, 3

2. small_values.py (the full counterexample-guided SAT search, both
   directions: witness colourings AND the unsatisfiability proof that no
   better colouring exists) was independently re-run from scratch for
   n <= 6 and reproduced the paper's table exactly, for both f and F:
     n:  1  2  3  4  5  6
     f:  1  2  2  3  3  4
     F:  1  2  2  3  4  5

3. For n = 7, 8, 9 (f) and n = 3..7 (F), where a full from-scratch rerun
   of the harder (lower-bound / unsatisfiability) direction was too slow
   to redo within the time available, the existing witness colourings
   (witness_f_7.txt, witness_f_8.txt, witness_f_9.txt, witness_F_3.txt
   through witness_F_7.txt) were independently re-checked against the
   paper's own independent SAT verifier (the cardinality-constraint
   encoding in witnesses.py, which shares no code with the
   branch-and-bound search in small_values.py). All passed: none of
   these colourings contains a monochromatic closed family larger than
   claimed.

4. The proof of Theorem 1.2 (F(n) <= exp(O((log n)^2)), which closes
   Erdos's second conjecture on Problem 1183) was read line by line and
   checked to be a correct application of the probabilistic deletion
   method together with the Sauer-Shelah lemma; no gap was found.

No fabricated or hardcoded results were found anywhere in this code,
unlike a defect found and corrected in an earlier, unrelated paper in
this series. What remains open -- Erdos's first conjecture in general,
and the order of magnitude of f(n) -- is stated as open in the paper
itself and was not attempted here; see the paper's Section 9 ("Summary
of what is proved and what is open") and Section 10 ("Questions") for a
precise account, including Remark 10.4 (poset Ramsey numbers),
Remark 10.5 (block-generated Boolean subalgebras) and Remark 10.6
together with Lemma 10.6 (the doubling recursion), each of which proves
that a specific natural strategy for the first conjecture does not
work, and none of which claims to close it.

5. Re-verification for the present submission (September 2026): every
   witness file supplied with the code (witness_f_3.txt through
   witness_f_9.txt, witness_F_3.txt through witness_F_7.txt) was
   re-checked, from the files as shipped, against the independent SAT
   verifier in witnesses.py, for the exact values printed in the
   paper's Theorem 1.4 table:
     f(3..9) = 2,3,3,4,4,5,5  and  F(3..7) = 2,3,4,5,7.
   Every check passed for both colour classes of every n. brute_small.py
   was also re-run and again reproduced f(n) = F(n) = 1,2,2,3 for
   n = 1,2,3,4. All bibliography DOIs were independently re-verified
   online against the publisher or journal landing page for each entry
   (title, authors, journal, volume and page range checked to match the
   bibliography item); no discrepancy was found.
